🌿 Peoples of the Verdigris Isles

Humans

Adaptable and widespread, humans can be found throughout nearly every settlement and region of the Verdigris Isles. Rather than being defined by a single ancestry or homeland, humanity is shaped by the cultures, communities, and environments in which its people live.

✦ Identity

A People Defined by Where They Belong

Humans are among the most widespread peoples of the Verdigris Isles. They can be found in nearly every major settlement, region, and community throughout the archipelago.

Unlike peoples whose identities are closely tied to a particular ancestral lineage, humans are largely defined by their homeland, culture, community, and upbringing.

A human raised along the Azure Coast may have a dramatically different worldview, appearance, traditions, and way of life from a human raised in the Kharad Highlands or among the streets of Verde Haven.

There is no single human culture. Humanity's greatest defining trait is its ability to adapt to the world around it.

✦ Human Homelands

Many Homes. One People.

Human identity is often closely connected to the place where a person was raised. Geography, local history, and community traditions can shape human culture as strongly as ancestry shapes other peoples.

Frontier

Frontier communities may be shaped heavily by the surrounding landscape and the challenges of living beyond established centers.

✦ Overview & Origins

Humanity Across the Isles

Humans have existed throughout the Verdigris Isles for generations, becoming one of the most geographically widespread peoples in the known world.

Their history is deeply intertwined with the development of the settlements, kingdoms, trade routes, and communities that now make up the modern Isles.

The fall of the Citadel and the changes that followed did not erase humanity's ability to adapt. Instead, human communities continued to rebuild, migrate, trade, and establish new lives throughout the fractured world that remained.

As a result, there is no single human homeland that defines the entire population. Human identity is instead shaped by countless local histories.

✦ Cultures of the Known World

Humanity Shaped by Where You Call Home

Humans are not defined by a single culture. Across the known world, human societies have developed distinct traditions, values, industries, and ways of life shaped by their environments and histories.

🌊

Thalassan

Southwest

Thalassan culture developed around prosperous port cities and the sea. Commerce, navigation, exploration, and maritime trade are deeply woven into everyday life.

Thalassans tend to place great value on independence, commercial opportunity, practical knowledge, and the ability to navigate both the seas and the complicated world of trade.

Known For:
Merchant guilds · Naval exploration · Shipbuilding · Banking · Cartography

⛰️

Kharad

Northwest

Kharad culture comes from the rugged mountain kingdoms of the Highlands, where survival, craftsmanship, and engineering have shaped society for generations.

Kharad people often take pride in practical skill and mastery of their chosen craft. Builders, smiths, engineers, and artisans are widely respected.

Known For:
Steelworking · Engineering · Heavy armor · Fortress cities · Mechanical craftsmanship

🌿

Ardynwood

Northeast

Ardynwood culture grew alongside enormous forests rather than replacing them. Communities are deeply connected to the natural world, with settlements built into and around living ecosystems.

Local councils, wardens, herbalists, and naturalists play important roles within society, while traditions surrounding spirits and the preservation of nature remain influential.

Known For:
Herbal medicine · Botanical research · Spirit traditions · Living architecture · Wildlife conservation

☀️

Heliora

North

Heliora culture developed within prosperous desert city-states where scholarship, mathematics, astronomy, and magical research became central to society.

Education and intellectual achievement are highly valued, with universities, observatories, libraries, and centers of research preserving knowledge from across the known world.

Known For:
Universities · Astronomy · Jewelcraft · Libraries · Glassmaking

🌺

Azure

East

Azure culture is rooted in tropical island life and generations of maritime tradition. Communities maintain close relationships with the ocean and the resources it provides.

Cooperation is an important part of Azure culture. Sailing, fishing, pearl diving, and ocean stewardship are traditions passed between generations.

Known For:
Sailing · Pearl diving · Coral architecture · Fishing · Ocean traditions

🐾

Talanor

Southeast

Talanor culture developed along a rugged frontier where survival and exploration are necessities rather than luxuries.

Rangers, scouts, hunters, and explorers are highly respected, and knowledge of the wilderness is considered one of the most valuable skills a person can possess.

Known For:
Frontier exploration · Monster hunting · Survival training · Falconry · Uncharted cartography

🏛️

Caelmont

West

Caelmont culture is centered around noble courts, influential families, diplomacy, and carefully maintained social reputation.

Courtly life places considerable importance on etiquette, patronage, family connections, political alliances, and social standing. Art, fashion, and luxury craftsmanship are equally important expressions of prestige.

Known For:
Noble houses · Court diplomacy · Fine arts · Luxury craftsmanship · Political influence

✦ Lifespan & Biology

A Familiar People in a Changing World

Humans possess a broadly familiar humanoid biology and do not possess the specialized physical adaptations associated with many other peoples of the Verdigris Isles.

Their greatest biological advantage is their general adaptability. Humans can live in a wide variety of environments, from major cities and coastal settlements to rural communities and frontier regions.

Human lifespans and physical characteristics vary naturally between individuals and populations. However, a typical human lifespan is 80–100 years.

Unlike certain other peoples, human identity is not defined by a particular magical, aquatic, animal, or elemental ancestry.
