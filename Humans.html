    Humans

    One people. Countless cultures. Across the Verdigris Isles, Human identity is shaped less by bloodline and more by the place they call home.

    🌍
    Culture is home.

    A Human raised in the mountains may have an entirely different worldview from one raised beside the sea, in the capital, or among Talanor's monsters.

    Overview & Origins

    A People Shaped by Place

    Humans are among the most widespread peoples of the Verdigris Isles, found in nearly every nation, settlement, and environment across the archipelago.

    Unlike peoples whose identities are strongly tied to a particular ancestry, transformation, or environment, Humans possess relatively few inherent physical traits that distinguish them from one another. Their greatest diversity comes from culture, homeland, occupation, and upbringing.

    A Human raised among the mountain foundries of Kharad may have an entirely different worldview from one raised along the beaches of the Azure Coast. A Human born in Verde Haven may grow up surrounded by trade, politics, guilds, and relic hunters, while someone raised in the Ardynwood may be far more familiar with forests, spirits, hunting, and wilderness travel.

    Human identity is often defined less by bloodline and more by place.
    A homeland may influence accent, language, clothing, cuisine, religious practices, family traditions, social expectations, occupation, architecture and lifestyle, relationship with Aether and technology, attitudes toward other peoples, and ideas of family, community, and authority.

    These are cultural influences rather than universal rules. A Human from one nation may move elsewhere, adopt another culture, or grow up between multiple communities. Humans are among the most culturally adaptable peoples of the Isles.

    Humans have existed throughout the Verdigris Isles for longer than most surviving records can reliably document. Their earliest origins are debated. Some traditions claim Humans were among the first peoples to settle the Isles, while others suggest they arrived through ancient migrations that occurred long before the Sundering.

    Humans have demonstrated an extraordinary ability to establish themselves almost anywhere. They have built settlements along coastlines, established cities around ancient ruins, developed communities in wetlands, carved homes into mountain valleys, and reclaimed abandoned structures from the Ascension Era.

    Today, Humans can be found throughout nearly every major nation and region. Their cultures are therefore extremely diverse. There is no single Human culture. Instead, there are many Human cultures shaped by the history and environment of the communities in which they developed.

    Physical Characteristics

    The Human Baseline

    Humans possess the most consistent physical baseline among the major peoples of the Verdigris Isles.

    • Two arms and two legs
    • Humanoid anatomy
    • Five-fingered hands
    • A wide range of skin tones
    • A wide range of hair colors and textures
    • A wide range of eye colors
    • Highly variable heights and builds

    Human physical appearance is influenced primarily by ancestry and family genetics rather than magical lineage.

    Unlike Beastials, Humans do not naturally possess animal traits. Unlike Aelori, they do not possess inherent aquatic adaptations. Unlike Duvara, they do not naturally transform between forms.

    Individual Humans may develop unusual characteristics through magical exposure, Aether influence, ancestry, injury, or other circumstances. These exceptions are not considered representative of Humans as a whole.

    Culture & Society

    Many Cultures, One Race

    Human societies are extremely diverse. There is no universal Human religion, government, tradition, or social structure shared by every Human community.

    Family → Homeland → Nation → Profession → Personal Identity
    A Human may consider themselves a citizen of Caelmont, a member of a particular town, part of a specific family, and a member of a guild simultaneously.

    Migration is also common. Humans frequently move between nations for work, trade, marriage, education, exploration, military service, political reasons, or personal opportunity.

    Because of this, Human cultural identity can become complicated.

    Talanor Dominion

    Monster territory / Dangerous frontier

    Talanor is known throughout the Verdigris Isles as the Land of Monsters. Ancient forests, enormous wetlands, ruined settlements, strange caverns, and unexplored territories make Talanor one of the most dangerous regions in the Isles.

    Cultural Influences
    • Monster ecology
    • Survival
    • Wilderness travel
    • Monster tracking
    • Defensive settlements
    • Expedition culture
    • Relic exploration
    • Beast lore
    • Frontier communities
    Common Traits

    Humans raised in Talanor often develop a practical understanding of monsters from an early age. Preparedness is valued; bravery is respected, but recklessness is not. Many settlements use watchtowers, reinforced walls, warning systems, and evacuation routes.

    Relationship With Monsters

    Some monsters are dangerous pests or predators. Others are respected as part of the natural world. Certain intelligent creatures maintain trade or diplomatic relationships with Human settlements, while others remain hostile.

    Lifespan & Biology

    Adaptable by Nature

    80–100

    Typical Lifespan

    Humans generally live between 80 and 100 years, though some individuals may live longer due to exceptional health, magical influence, Aether exposure, or other circumstances.

    No Required Adaptation

    Humans possess no inherent supernatural transformation ability or environmental adaptation.

    Versatility

    Humans can generally survive in a wide range of environments with appropriate shelter, food, water, and resources.

    Character Creation

    Build a Human

    Your character's homeland should give you a foundation for their history and upbringing, not dictate their personality or future.

    Culture provides a foundation, not a destiny.

    Where a Human comes from may shape who they become—but it does not decide who they are.

    1. Race: Human
    2. Homeland: Where your character was raised
    3. Nation: The nation or political region they identify with
    4. Cultural Background: The traditions and communities that influenced their upbringing
    5. Family Heritage: Consider whether their family has lived in the region for generations or migrated from elsewhere
    6. Occupation: What they do within society
    7. Personal Appearance: Skin, hair, eyes, height, build, and other physical characteristics
    8. Languages/Dialects: Consider how their homeland influences their speech
    9. Relationship With Aether: Decide whether they have ordinary familiarity with Aether, formal training, or little exposure
