✦ People of the Verdigris Isles

Humans

A widespread and adaptable people whose identities are shaped not by a single ancestry, but by homeland, culture, family, occupation, and the communities in which they live.

“Where a Human comes from may shape who they become — but it does not decide who they are.”
Identity

A People Defined by Place

Humans are among the most widespread peoples of the Verdigris Isles, found in nearly every nation, settlement, and environment across the archipelago.

Unlike peoples whose identities are strongly tied to a particular ancestry, transformation, or environment, Humans possess relatively few inherent physical traits that distinguish them from one another. Their greatest diversity comes from culture, homeland, occupation, and upbringing.

A Human raised among the mountain foundries of Kharad may have an entirely different worldview from one raised along the beaches of the Azure Coast.

A Human born in Verde Haven may grow up surrounded by trade, politics, guilds, and relic hunters, while someone raised in Ardynwood may be far more familiar with forests, spirits, hunting, and wilderness travel.

Because of this, Human identity is often defined less by bloodline and more by place.

🧭
A Human's homeland may influence who they become without determining who they are.

Overview & Origins

Adaptable Across the Isles

Humans have existed throughout the Verdigris Isles for longer than most surviving records can reliably document.

Their earliest origins are debated. Some traditions claim Humans were among the first peoples to settle the Isles, while others suggest they arrived through ancient migrations that occurred long before the Sundering.

Unlike some peoples whose origins are closely associated with particular environments, Humans have demonstrated an extraordinary ability to establish themselves almost anywhere.

They have built settlements along coastlines, established cities around ancient ruins, developed communities in wetlands, carved homes into mountain valleys, and reclaimed abandoned structures from the Ascension Era.

This adaptability has allowed Humans to become one of the most widespread peoples in the Isles.

Today, Humans can be found throughout nearly every major nation and region. Their cultures are therefore extremely diverse. There is no single Human culture.

🌎
There is no single Human culture — only countless cultures shaped by the places Humans call home.
Physical Characteristics

The Most Familiar Form

Humans possess the most consistent physical baseline among the major peoples of the Verdigris Isles.

Common Characteristics

  • Two arms and two legs
  • Humanoid anatomy
  • Five-fingered hands
  • A wide range of skin tones
  • A wide range of hair colors and textures
  • A wide range of eye colors
  • Highly variable heights and builds

What Humans Lack

Unlike Beastials, Humans do not naturally possess animal traits.

Unlike Aelori, they do not possess inherent aquatic adaptations.

Unlike Duvara, they do not naturally transform between forms.

Their physical appearance is influenced primarily by ancestry and family genetics rather than magical lineage.

Exceptions

Individual Humans may develop unusual characteristics through magical exposure, Aether influence, ancestry, injury, or other circumstances. These exceptions are not considered representative of Humans as a whole.

Culture & Society

Identity Is Layered

Human societies are extremely diverse. There is no universal Human religion, government, tradition, or social structure shared by every Human community.

Humans generally identify with several overlapping communities:

Family → Homeland → Nation → Profession → Personal Identity

A Human may consider themselves a citizen of Caelmont, a member of a particular town, part of a specific family, and a member of a guild simultaneously.

Migration is also common. Humans frequently move between nations for work, trade, marriage, education, exploration, military service, political reasons, or personal opportunity.

Because of this, Human cultural identity can become complicated.

🏠
A person can belong to more than one place.
Homelands & Cultures

Humanity Across the Isles

Human culture changes dramatically depending upon where a person is raised. These regional identities provide cultural foundations, not rigid character archetypes.

Capital Culture

🌿 Verde Haven

Humans raised in Verde Haven are surrounded by one of the most diverse populations in the Isles.

As the capital and largest metropolitan hub, Verde Haven brings together merchants, guild workers, politicians, artisans, travelers, scholars, and adventurers from across the archipelago.

Growing up in Verde Haven often means becoming comfortable around people from vastly different cultures and backgrounds.

Court Culture

🏛️ Caelmont

Human culture in Caelmont is heavily influenced by its courtly and noble traditions.

Social position, etiquette, family reputation, education, and political relationships can play an important role in daily life.

Monster Territory

🐉 Talanor Dominion

Talanor is known throughout the Verdigris Isles as the Land of Monsters.

Unlike most regions, where settlements were built around the assumption that Humans and other civilized peoples were the dominant inhabitants, Talanor developed in a land where monstrous creatures have always been part of the natural environment.

Ancient forests, enormous wetlands, ruined settlements, strange caverns, and unexplored territories make Talanor one of the most dangerous regions in the Isles.

Cultural Influences

  • Monster ecology
  • Survival
  • Wilderness travel
  • Monster tracking
  • Defensive settlements
  • Expedition culture
  • Relic exploration
  • Beast lore
  • Frontier communities

Common Traits

Humans raised in Talanor often develop a practical understanding of monsters from an early age.

Children may learn to recognize tracks, identify dangerous calls, understand territorial markings, and recognize which creatures should be avoided rather than fought.

Talanor culture tends to place considerable value on preparedness. Being brave is respected, but being reckless is not.

Land of Monsters

In Talanor, monsters are not simply rare threats encountered by adventurers. They are part of the ecosystem — predators, territorial creatures, intelligent peoples, and ancient beings whose origins remain unknown.

Talanor Dominion

Survival Through Understanding

Talanorian Humans have complicated relationships with the creatures around them.

Relationship With Monsters

Some monsters are considered dangerous pests or predators. Others are respected as part of the natural world.

Certain intelligent creatures maintain trade or diplomatic relationships with Human settlements, while others remain hostile.

Monster hunters and researchers often travel deep into Talanor to study creatures that cannot be found anywhere else in the Isles.

Preparedness

Many settlements are heavily fortified, with watchtowers, reinforced walls, warning systems, and designated evacuation routes.

A successful Talanorian knows when to fight, when to hide, and when to leave an area entirely.

Lifespan & Biology

Adaptable by Nature

Humans typically mature at roughly the same rate as modern humans and generally live between 80 and 100 years.

Some individuals may live longer due to exceptional health, magical influence, Aether exposure, or other circumstances.

Humans possess no inherent supernatural transformation ability or environmental adaptation.

Their greatest biological advantage is their versatility.

Humans can generally survive in a wide range of environments provided they have access to appropriate shelter, food, water, and resources.

🌱
Humans do not belong to one environment. They make homes wherever they can.
