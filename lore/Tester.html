INTRODUCTION

The End of an Age

The fall of the Citadel was not a single moment. It was a process that had already begun long before the city finally came down.

For generations, the Citadel had represented the highest achievement of Ascension civilization. Its towers rose above the surrounding world, powered by technologies that earlier generations could scarcely have imagined.

Aetherite made those achievements possible. It powered the engines, stabilized the systems, and provided the foundation upon which the civilization's extraordinary infrastructure had been built.

But the resource that had allowed civilization to reach such heights could not be replaced.

HISTORICAL NOTE

The term 0 FC refers specifically to the physical fall of the Citadel.

02
THE OLD WORLD

The Citadel

Before the fall, the Citadel was more than a city. It was the center of an entire civilization.

Industry, scholarship, commerce, transportation, and magical research converged within its walls. The city's influence extended far beyond the structure itself, reaching into territories that depended upon its machines, its knowledge, and its resources.

The great Ascension Engines formed the heart of this system. They transformed Aetherite into usable power and allowed the Citadel to accomplish feats that had once belonged only to myth.

03
THE ASCENSION ERA

The Age of Ascension

The civilization that eventually fell had once seemed incapable of failure.

The discovery and refinement of Aetherite changed the trajectory of the world. What had previously been isolated magical practices could now be incorporated into machinery, infrastructure, and industry.

Cities expanded. Transportation became faster. Communication crossed distances that had once separated entire regions. Factories operated on systems of power that seemed almost limitless.

The Citadel became the greatest expression of this philosophy.

BEFORE 0 FC

The Aetherite Decline

The first signs of collapse were small enough to dismiss.

Aetherite reserves began to diminish. Engines required increasingly frequent maintenance. Infrastructure became unreliable, and the systems responsible for distributing power could no longer operate at their former capacity.

At first, the problems were treated as temporary shortages. Production would recover. New reserves would be discovered. Engineers would find solutions.

That belief lasted until the shortages became impossible to hide.

THE COLLAPSE BEGINS

The Final Days

By the final days of the Citadel, daily life had changed dramatically.

Resources were rationed. Power became increasingly unreliable. Entire sections of infrastructure operated intermittently or ceased functioning altogether.

The population had begun to understand that something fundamental was wrong.

Yet the Citadel remained standing.

Its towers still dominated the horizon. Its remaining engines still turned. Its institutions continued to insist that the crisis could be contained.

HISTORICAL OBSERVATION

Later accounts often describe the final period as peaceful immediately before the fall. Surviving records suggest otherwise.

THE YEAR OF THE FALL 0 FC
THE CITADEL

Fell.

Whatever remained of the old system failed in a single catastrophic event.

The Citadel descended from the sky.

Its fall destroyed the city and shattered the civilization that had depended upon it. Structures that had stood for generations were torn apart. Machinery, archives, homes, laboratories, and entire districts were buried beneath the ruins.

The impact permanently altered the surrounding landscape.

0 FC marks the physical fall of the Citadel.
07
AFTER THE FALL

The World After

The world did not end with the Citadel.

It changed.

Survivors scattered into the territories that remained habitable. Communities formed around whatever resources could be recovered. Old technologies were salvaged, repaired, and repurposed.

The civilization that followed was not a continuation of the old world so much as an attempt to understand what remained of it.

Ruins became settlements. Relics became tools. Forgotten machines became the foundation for new inventions.

The old world had fallen.

The next one had begun.
