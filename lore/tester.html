01
HISTORICAL OVERVIEW

Introduction

The Citadel was once the greatest achievement of the Ascendant Age. It represented the height of civilization: a place where magic, engineering, scholarship, and industry existed together on a scale the modern world can scarcely comprehend.

Its towers reached above the surrounding world, powered by vast reserves of Aetherite and sustained by systems that had taken generations to perfect. From the Citadel came discoveries, machines, magical disciplines, and technologies that reshaped civilization.

Yet the Citadel's greatness depended upon a resource that could not be replaced.

When the world's Aetherite reserves began to fail, the foundations of the Ascendant Age began to fail with them.

THE OLD WORLD "The Citadel did not fall in a single moment. Its fall had already begun."
02
BEFORE 0 FC

The Final Days

In the period immediately preceding the Fall, the Citadel had entered a state of increasing instability.

Aetherite reserves were becoming critically depleted. Systems that had once operated continuously began failing without warning. Power became unreliable, infrastructure deteriorated, and resources were increasingly rationed.

The people of the Citadel did not initially understand that they were witnessing the end of an age.

For generations, the city had been built around the assumption that its systems would continue functioning. When those systems began to fail, there was no simple replacement for them.

HISTORICAL NOTE

The period immediately preceding 0 FC is generally referred to as the Final Days of the Citadel. The year 0 FC marks the physical Fall itself, rather than the beginning of the collapse.

As shortages worsened, the distinction between ordinary maintenance failures and systemic collapse became increasingly difficult to maintain.

03
THE COLLAPSE BEGINS

A Civilization Losing Power

The Citadel's dependence upon Aetherite had always been one of its greatest strengths. It had allowed its engineers and arcanists to accomplish feats that earlier civilizations could only imagine.

It eventually became one of its greatest weaknesses.

As Aetherite reserves dwindled, the systems supporting the Citadel became increasingly unreliable. Power networks faltered. Industrial operations slowed. Transportation systems became difficult to maintain.

The city began rationing what remained.

What had once been abundant became valuable. What had once been routine became a privilege.

◆
THE AETHERITE CRISIS

The depletion of Aetherite was not simply an energy shortage. It represented the loss of the fundamental resource upon which the Ascendant civilization had been constructed.

Attempts were made to preserve essential systems. Some districts were prioritized over others. Industrial facilities were shut down or consolidated. Nonessential infrastructure was abandoned.

The Citadel was no longer expanding. It was attempting to survive.

04
0 FC

The Fall

The Fall of the Citadel was not merely the destruction of a city. It was the collapse of the central structure upon which the old world depended.

At some point during the final crisis, the Citadel's systems failed beyond recovery.

The immense structure could no longer maintain itself. Infrastructure collapsed. Sections of the city broke apart, and the Citadel came down upon the land beneath it.

The event permanently altered the surrounding region.

0 FC

The year remembered not for what began, but for what finally fell.

The physical destruction of the Citadel became the defining event of the new era. Survivors scattered into the world beyond the ruins, carrying fragments of knowledge, technology, culture, and memory with them.

The age of Ascension was over.

05
AFTER THE FALL

The Aftermath

The world that emerged from the disaster was fragmented. Communities that had once depended upon the Citadel were forced to become self-sufficient.

Old roads became dangerous. Ruined facilities became sources of valuable materials. Forgotten machines were stripped for parts. Ancient structures were repurposed rather than rebuilt.

Over generations, survivors learned to live among the remains of the civilization that had come before them.

Some ruins became settlements.

Some became graveyards.

Others became places that people were simply wise enough to leave alone.

THE CROWNSCAR

The ruins created by the Citadel's destruction became known as the Crownscar, a vast region where remnants of the old civilization remain buried, exposed, and waiting to be rediscovered.

06
THE WORLD THAT REMAINS

Legacy

The Citadel is gone, but its influence remains everywhere.

Modern civilization is built among its remnants. Old machines continue to shape new inventions. Ruins provide materials, knowledge, and dangers in equal measure. Ancient techniques are being rediscovered by people who no longer possess the context in which they were originally created.

This period of reconstruction has given rise to a new age: a civilization piecing together the achievements of the past while attempting not to repeat its mistakes.

The people of the Verdigris Isles live in the shadow of that history.

They inherit its ruins.

They salvage its machines.

They tell stories about its wonders.

And somewhere beneath the earth, beneath forests and marshes and cities built upon forgotten foundations, the old world still waits.
