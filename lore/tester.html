HISTORY · THE OLD WORLD

The Fall of the Citadel

The end of the old world was not a single event. It was the culmination of centuries of expansion, dependence, decline, and desperation.

I

Overview

For centuries, the Citadel stood at the center of civilization. It was a city built upon accumulated knowledge, immense magical infrastructure, and the increasingly sophisticated use of Aetherite.

Its influence reached far beyond its walls. Nations depended upon Citadel technology, merchants depended upon its trade networks, and entire populations lived according to systems whose origins had become so old that few questioned them.

The Citadel appeared permanent.

It was not.

✦
II

Before the Fall

The final collapse did not begin in the year remembered as the Fall. By the time the Citadel finally came crashing down, the foundations of its civilization had already been failing for years.

Aetherite reserves had become increasingly difficult to obtain. The great engines required enormous quantities of the crystal, while the remaining deposits became smaller, deeper, and more dangerous to reach.

HISTORICAL NOTE

Later generations would describe the Fall as sudden. Contemporary records suggest something considerably more complicated: the Citadel was already dying.

The response was not immediate abandonment. Instead, the Citadel attempted to preserve the systems that remained.

III

The Collapse Begins

As Aetherite reserves diminished, the infrastructure of the Citadel became increasingly unreliable.

Power was rationed. Transportation networks failed intermittently. Industrial districts were forced to reduce production, while essential systems were given priority over civilian needs.

What had once been invisible became impossible to ignore.

The lights of the upper city were the first to disappear.

— Fragment attributed to a pre-Fall municipal record

The wealthy withdrew deeper into protected districts while the lower settlements absorbed increasing numbers of displaced workers and families.

IV

The Final Days

In the final period before the Fall, the Citadel ceased functioning as a unified city.

Entire districts were isolated from one another. Food and water were rationed. Mechanical systems were stripped for parts, and increasingly desperate attempts were made to keep the Ascension infrastructure operating.

Among the last functioning areas were portions of the elite gardens and residential districts. When the city finally failed, sections of those elevated structures collapsed into the settlements below.

Centuries later, those ruins would become part of the foundations upon which modern Verde Haven was built.

V

The Fall

0 FC marks the physical Fall of the Citadel.

Whatever sequence of failures ultimately brought the city down remains disputed. Surviving accounts disagree on the final hours, and many of the records that could have resolved the matter were destroyed alongside the city.

What is certain is that the Citadel did not simply lose power.

It fell.

Towers, infrastructure, gardens, residential districts, and the machinery that had sustained the old civilization were thrown into ruin. The devastation reshaped the surrounding land and scattered the remnants of Citadel civilization across the region.

VI

The Aftermath

The generations that followed inherited a world they no longer understood.

The great systems of the Citadel were broken, but they were not entirely forgotten. Relics, machines, fragments of knowledge, and strange magical materials remained scattered throughout the ruins.

Over generations, people learned to salvage what they could.

The civilization that emerged from those efforts would not recreate the old world.

It would become something new.
