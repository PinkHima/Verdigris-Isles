The final days of the civilization that once stood at the height of Ascension, and the catastrophe that reshaped the world that followed.

History The Old World 0 FC
01 / FINAL DAYS

The Final Days of the Citadel

Before the Fall, the Citadel stood as the greatest expression of civilization the world had ever known. Its towers reached into the sky, its engines carried entire structures above the earth, and its streets were sustained by systems that had become so deeply integrated into daily life that few could imagine existence without them.

Beneath that prosperity, however, the foundations of the Ascension age were beginning to fail. Aetherite reserves were becoming critically depleted. Infrastructure grew increasingly unreliable, and the systems responsible for maintaining the Citadel's extraordinary scale required more resources than the civilization could continue to provide.

Historical Note

The final years should not be understood as a single moment of collapse. The Fall was preceded by a prolonged period of shortages, failing infrastructure, rationing, and growing uncertainty.

Resources were rationed. Industrial production slowed. Repairs that would once have been routine became increasingly difficult as replacement components and refined Aetherite became scarce.

02 / COLLAPSE

The Collapse Begins

The first failures were isolated. A transport line would stop without warning. A district would lose power for several hours. An industrial engine would require components that could no longer be manufactured.

Individually, these incidents were survivable. Together, they represented something far more serious: the gradual unraveling of the systems upon which the Citadel depended.

“The Citadel did not fall in a single day. By the time the end came, much of it had already begun to disappear.”

As failures multiplied, entire districts became increasingly isolated. Food distribution became unreliable. Manufacturing centers struggled to remain operational. The great infrastructure networks that had once connected the Citadel began to fragment.

03 / 0 FC

The Fall

0 FC · THE FALL OF THE CITADEL

The final catastrophe came when the systems holding the Citadel aloft could no longer maintain their intended function.

What followed became known simply as the Fall. Structures broke apart. Entire sections of the Citadel collapsed from the sky, carrying with them homes, infrastructure, machinery, archives, and countless remnants of the civilization that had created them.

The physical destruction was immense, but the consequences extended far beyond the wreckage. The Fall severed the networks that had connected distant settlements and shattered the political order that had depended upon the Citadel's continued existence.

On the Calendar

0 FC refers specifically to the physical Fall of the Citadel itself. The shortages, political instability, and infrastructure failures immediately preceding the disaster belong to the period before 0 FC.

04 / AFTERMATH

The World After

The world that emerged from the Fall was not the world that had existed before it.

Survivors gathered around whatever fragments of civilization remained. Settlements formed beside ruins, old roads became new trade routes, and forgotten technologies were dismantled for parts that could no longer be manufactured.

Generations passed. Knowledge that had once been commonplace became specialized, fragmented, or lost. The ruins of the old civilization became both dangerous remnants and invaluable sources of technology.

From this period emerged the foundations of the modern Verdigris Isles: a civilization built not upon the untouched legacy of the Old World, but upon the difficult work of reclaiming it.
